# Form footer

The row of buttons at the end of a form. It looks trivial and is where most forms go wrong.

<div className="dx"><div className="dx-demo"><div className="dx-actions">
<Button label="Cancel" emphasis="tertiary" />
<Button label="Save draft" emphasis="secondary" />
<Button label="Save policy" emphasis="primary" />
</div></div></div>

**Components used:** Button (tertiary, secondary, primary). **Tokens:** inherited from Button. See its page for the full list.

## The rules

1. **Right aligned, least to most important.** Left to right: tertiary, secondary, primary. The primary is always the rightmost button.
2. **One primary, and it names the outcome.** "Save policy," not "Submit."
3. **Use as many as the form needs, and no more.** Two actions: Cancel is secondary, right beside the primary. Three: Cancel drops to tertiary on the far left, and the alternative (Save draft) becomes the secondary in the middle.
4. **Same size, same row.** Do not mix sizes in a group.
5. **On a phone, stack full width in the same order.** The primary ends up last, closest to the thumb, and keyboard and screen reader order still matches what people see.
6. **Do not disable Save to hide a problem.** Leave it enabled and show a clear error on the field that needs fixing. A disabled button cannot be focused or explain itself. This is our rule, not a standard.
7. **Saving shows progress.** Use the Button `loading` state. The label and width stay the same, and screen readers hear "Loading."

## Do and don't

<Button label="Save policy" />

<DoDont ok={false} title="Primary first, or two primaries" why="Moving the primary around makes people re-learn each screen. Two loud buttons make them stop and read.">

<Button label="Save draft" />
<Button label="Cancel" emphasis="tertiary" />
</div>
</DoDont>
</div>

## Edge cases to design for

- A long form: repeat the footer as a sticky bar so Save is always reachable.
- A save that can fail: keep the person's input, say what happened in plain words, and let them retry.
- Unsaved changes on Cancel: confirm with a dialog (see Destructive confirmation).

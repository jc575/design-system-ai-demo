# Form footer

The row of buttons at the end of a form. It looks trivial and is where most forms go wrong.

<div className="dx"><div className="dx-demo">
<Button label="Cancel" emphasis="secondary" />
<Button label="Save policy" emphasis="primary" />
</div></div>

**Components used:** Button (primary, secondary). **Tokens:** inherited from Button. See its page for the full list.

## The rules

1. **One primary, and it names the outcome.** "Save policy," not "Submit."
2. **Cancel is secondary, next to the primary.** It is a real choice, so it gets a border, but it does not outshine Save.
3. **Same size, same row.** Do not mix sizes in a group.
4. **Primary first or last? Pick one and keep it the same everywhere.** Carbon puts the primary on the left in full-page forms and on the right in dialogs, and we follow that.
5. **Do not disable Save to hide a problem.** Leave it enabled and show a clear error on the field that needs fixing. A disabled button cannot be focused or explain itself. This is our rule, not a standard.
6. **Saving shows progress.** Use the Button `loading` state. The label and width stay the same, and screen readers hear "Loading."

## Do and don't

<DoDont ok={false} title="Two primaries, vague labels" why="'Save' and 'Submit' next to each other make people stop and read. Decide which one matters.">
<Button label="Submit" />
<Button label="Save" />
</DoDont>

## Edge cases to design for

- A long form: repeat the footer as a sticky bar so Save is always reachable.
- A save that can fail: keep the person's input, say what happened in plain words, and let them retry.
- Unsaved changes on Cancel: confirm with a dialog (see Destructive confirmation).

# Elevation, borders and motion

## Elevation means order, not decoration

Elevation tells people what is in front of what. Five levels, named by **job**:

<Doc>

| Token | Job | Surface it pairs with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `--elevation-flat` | On the page | `--color-bg-page` |
| `--elevation-resting` | Cards and panels | `--color-bg-raised` |
| `--elevation-raised` | Hovered cards, sticky headers | `--color-bg-raised` |
| `--elevation-overlay` | Menus, popovers, tooltips | `--color-bg-overlay` |
| `--elevation-modal` | Dialogs that block the page | `--color-bg-overlay` |

### The dark mode problem

Shadows are almost invisible on dark surfaces. So in dark mode we do two things: shadows are stronger (they use the "deep" primitives, the light theme uses "soft" ones), and **the surface itself gets lighter as it comes forward**: page, then raised, then overlay. If you removed the shadows completely, the order would still be readable.

That is also why primitives are named by how they *look* (soft, deep) and not by theme. The theme decides which one to point at.

### Rules

- Use elevation to show a layer that is *actually* above another one.
- Do not stack more than two elevated layers.
- Never rely on a shadow alone to mark a boundary people must perceive. Give the thing a border or a fill change too.

## Borders and focus

- `--border-width-default` is 1px. `--border-width-emphasis` is 2px, used for error states.
- **Focus rings** are 2px with a 2px offset (`--focus-ring-width`, `--focus-ring-offset`) in `--color-border-focus`, which must reach 3:1 against the surface behind it. The contrast script checks it against page, raised and overlay in both themes.
- The border of an input or control uses `--color-border-strong` (3:1). The soft divider `--color-border-subtle` is for decoration only, and is not allowed on anything people must find.

## Motion

Two durations, both tokens: `--motion-duration-fast` (120ms, state changes) and `--motion-duration-spin` (700ms, loading spinners). When someone has asked their device to reduce motion, `base.css` shortens all animation and transition to near zero.

Motion is intentionally small in v1. Easing curves, enter and exit patterns and choreography would be the next foundation to add.

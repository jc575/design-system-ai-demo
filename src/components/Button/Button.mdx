# Button

A button says **"do this."** The question is never "which button looks nice?" It is "how important is this action, and what happens if someone clicks it by mistake?"

### The decision matrix

<Doc>

| Situation | Emphasis | Destructive? | Example label | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The main next step of a page, form or dialog | **Primary** | No | Save policy | One per view. If everything is primary, nothing is |
| The alternative or the way out, next to a primary | **Secondary** | No | Cancel | Always appears with a primary |
| A smaller or repeated action | **Tertiary** | No | Export CSV | Can stand alone. Good for table rows and toolbars |
| A quiet way out with no primary beside it | **Tertiary** | No | Cancel | Never competes with content |
| First click of a delete or remove flow | **Secondary** or **tertiary** | **Yes** | Delete rule | Quiet on purpose. Confirm in a dialog |
| Final confirm step of a destructive flow | **Primary** | **Yes** | Delete rule | The one place a destructive primary belongs |
| Going to another page | Not a button. **Use a link** | n/a | View policy details | Links can open in a new tab and announce as navigation |

</Doc>

## Real situations

### A form footer

<div className="dx"><div className="dx-demo"><div className="dx-actions">
<Button label="Cancel" emphasis="tertiary" />
<Button label="Save draft" emphasis="secondary" />
<Button label="Save policy" emphasis="primary" />
</div></div></div>

Right aligned, and read left to right from least to most important: tertiary, secondary, then the primary at the far right. With only two actions, Cancel is the secondary and sits next to the primary. With three, Cancel drops to tertiary on the far left. On a phone the group stacks full width in the same order, so the primary is last and closest to the thumb. See the Patterns page, Form footer.

### A destructive confirmation dialog

<div className="dx"><div className="dx-demo"><div className="dx-actions">
<Button label="Cancel" emphasis="secondary" />
<Button label="Delete rule" emphasis="primary" destructive iconStart={<Icon name="trash" size="sm" />} />
</div></div></div>

The dialog already told someone what will be deleted. Now the button repeats the action in plain words. "Delete rule" beats "OK" or "Yes" because it still makes sense if someone only reads the buttons.

<Button label="Edit" emphasis="tertiary" size="sm" />

Dozens of rows means dozens of buttons. Keep them quiet and small. The destructive one is red text with a word, and it asks for confirmation.

## Do and don't

<div className="dx-pair">
<DoDont ok title="One primary per view, at the far right" why="Carbon's rule on one primary, and ours on position. People should never wonder which action is the main one, and the main one is always where the group ends.">
<div className="dx-actions">
<Button label="Cancel" emphasis="secondary" />
<Button label="Save policy" />
</div>
</DoDont>
<DoDont ok={false} title="Two primaries side by side" why="Two loud choices cancel each other out. Decide which one matters more and demote the other.">
<Button label="Save draft" />
<Button label="Publish" />
</DoDont>
</div>

<div className="dx-pair">
<DoDont ok title="Say what will happen" why="Verb plus noun, in sentence case. It still makes sense out of context, for example in a screen reader's list of buttons.">
<Button label="Save policy" />
<Button label="Add rule" emphasis="secondary" />
</DoDont>
<DoDont ok={false} title="Vague labels" why="'Submit', 'OK' and 'Click here' make people read the surrounding text to learn what happens.">
<Button label="Submit" />
<Button label="OK" emphasis="secondary" />
</DoDont>
</div>

<div className="dx-pair">
<DoDont ok title="Make the first delete click quiet" why="A person reaching for a row action should not hit the loudest red button on the page by accident.">
<Button label="Delete rule" emphasis="secondary" destructive />
</DoDont>
<DoDont ok={false} title="A red primary as the page default" why="Unless the whole page is about deleting, a loud destructive primary invites mistakes and trains people to ignore red.">
<Button label="Delete rule" destructive />
<Button label="Save policy" emphasis="secondary" />
</DoDont>
</div>

<div className="dx-pair">
<DoDont ok title="Link for going somewhere" why="Links give people open in a new tab, a visible destination and the right screen reader announcement.">
<a href="#nav" onClick={(e) => e.preventDefault()} style={{ color: 'var(--color-text-link)', font: 'var(--type-label-md)' }}>View policy details</a>
</DoDont>
<DoDont ok={false} title="A button that navigates" why="Buttons do things on the current page. If it changes the URL, it is a link. (Carbon says the same.)">
<Button label="View policy details" emphasis="secondary" />
</DoDont>
</div>

## Rules in one list

1. **One primary per view.** Dialogs and side panels count as their own view.
2. **Secondary lives next to a primary.** On its own, use tertiary.
3. **Destructive is quiet until the last step.** The confirm step of a delete is the one place for a destructive primary.
4. **Verb plus noun, sentence case.** "Save policy," not "Submit" or "SAVE."
5. **Links navigate. Buttons act.**
6. **Do not mix sizes in a group.** Use medium by default, small in dense tables, large for touch-first or empty-state moments.
7. **Loading keeps the label and the width.** The button announces "Loading" to screen readers. Do not swap the label for a spinner alone.
8. **Disabled is a last resort.** A disabled button cannot be focused and cannot explain itself. Prefer leaving it enabled and showing a clear error on click. (This is our rule, not a standard. It trades a click for clarity.)
9. **Action groups are right aligned and read from least to most important, left to right: tertiary, secondary, primary.** The primary is always the rightmost button. The same order applies in form footers and dialogs. The one exception is centered content such as an empty state, where the invitation (the primary) comes first. This is our rule, chosen on purpose and written down in the Decision log (ADR-013).

## Accessibility

- Every button has a **visible text label**. v1 has no icon-only button on purpose.
- Focus ring: 2px, 3:1 against the surface, in both themes.
- All button text and fills are registered contrast pairs, including hover and pressed.
- Touch target: the medium button is 40px tall. WCAG 2.2 sets 24px as the AA minimum and recommends 44px, so use large for touch-first screens.
- The loading state sets `aria-busy` and says "Loading" to assistive tech.
